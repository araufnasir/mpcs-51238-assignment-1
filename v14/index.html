A Recipe for Changing a Price

What does a price change actually do?

Serves: anyone who sets or pays prices

Ingredients

  • 1 pricethe one you are thinking of changing
  • Your customerseveryone who might buy
  • Their alternativeswhat else they could buy, or do without
  • How much they need ita must-have, or a nice-to-have?

Method

  1. Change the price. Raise it a little, say by 10%.
  2. Watch demand react. When a price goes up, fewer people buy. When it goes down, more do. This always happens. Only the amount differs.
  3. Measure the reaction. How strongly buyers react has a name: price elasticity. A big reaction (cake, concert tickets) is elastic. A small reaction (petrol, medicine) is inelastic.
  4. Check your ingredients. Lots of alternatives and a nice-to-have: buyers leave fast. Few alternatives and a must-have: buyers stay.
  5. Taste the result. Raise an elastic price and you can earn less overall. Raise an inelastic price and you can earn more.

Chef's note

This is why a bakery runs sales while a petrol station hardly needs to, and why airlines charge more close to departure.

Before changing any price, ask: "If this cost more, what would my customers do instead?"
