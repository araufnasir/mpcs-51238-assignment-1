A short story about prices

One lemonade stand, two very different days

Raise a price and people buy less. The surprise is how much less, and why it differs. Follow one lemonade stand through two days.

1

A hot day, one stand

It is the day of a music festival and yours is the only lemonade stand for a mile. Each cup costs you $0.30 to make. What do you charge? Drag the price and see.

Notice that raising the price loses only a few customers. People are thirsty and have nowhere else to go.

2

A rival moves in

The next day another stand opens ten steps away, selling the same lemonade at the same cost. Now try the same price rise.

This time the same rise empties your queue. Buyers just walk next door.

What it means

How strongly buyers react to a price change has a name: price elasticity. When buyers react a lot, as with your rival next door, demand is elastic. When they hardly react, as at the festival, it is inelastic.

Three things usually decide it: whether there is something else to buy, whether it is a need or a wish, and how big a bite it takes from the budget.

That is why raising an elastic price can lower your income, and raising an inelastic one can lift it.

Before changing a price, ask: if this cost more, what would my customers do instead?
