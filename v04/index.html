The Price of Everything

Why a 10% rise empties one shop and barely dents another.

?

Imagine two shops on the same street. One sells fancy cupcakes, the other sells petrol. Both raise prices by ten percent on the same Monday. By Friday the cupcake counter is quiet. The petrol queue looks exactly as it did.

The gap between them is what economists call price elasticity: a measure of how strongly buyers react to a change in price.

“Would my customers leave, or just complain?”

Two kinds of buyer

Elastic demand means people react a lot. They can skip the cupcake, buy it next week, or buy a cookie instead. Inelastic demand means they react little. Nobody skips the commute because petrol went up a few pence.

Cupcakes, sales fall a lot
Petrol, sales barely move

What decides it

Three ingredients: substitutes (is there an easy alternative?), necessity (can you really do without?), and budget share (is it a large part of what you spend?).

Why it matters

Raise the price of something elastic and you may earn less overall. Raise the price of something inelastic and you may earn more. Knowing which one you sell is the difference between a smart price rise and an expensive mistake.
