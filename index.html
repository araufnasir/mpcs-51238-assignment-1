What does a price change actually do?

Twenty-five takes on one landing page explaining price elasticity in plain language. Versions 1–12 explore very different directions; 13–25 converge on what works. Jump to the final choice.

How I got here

  1. The idea. A plain-language explainer, "What does a price change actually do?", for non-economists: small business owners, students, anyone who sets or pays prices.
  2. Exploration (v01–v12). Twelve deliberately different directions: newspaper, brutalist manifesto, stock ticker, magazine, receipt, lemonade game, Swiss grid, 80s flyer, calm essay, picture book, dashboard and whiteboard.
  3. Narrowing. I picked v06, the interactive lemonade game, as the core because visitors learn by doing, and v12 for its approachable hand-drawn tone as the starting look for v13–v18. I dropped the dense or flashy directions (dashboard, brutalist, 80s flyer) because they looked striking but explained less.
  4. Pushback. v13–v17 shared one stylesheet, so they looked too similar. I caught it and had the agent give each its own visual world: arcade, split screen, chalkboard and ledger, plus a new v14.
  5. Dead end. The first v14, a blueprint, showed white text on a white page. A background shorthand I had written was invalid, but fixing it did not clear the problem, so I dropped it. A bus-fare poster came next, and I settled on a simple static recipe card.
  6. Convergence (v19–v25).
    • v19 combines v06's lemonade stand with v18's giant slider and a one-line coach; the lesson appears once you have pushed the price up in both situations.
    • v20 is a two-scene scrolling story with a "best possible profit" meter.
    • v21 puts the festival and rival stands side by side on one slider, so you see that no single price suits both.
    • v22 is a price lab: presets and dials for price, cost and how replaceable the product is, plus a "raise it another 10%" what-if.
    • v23 is a four-step tour: festival, rival, a guess-first petrol-vs-cake round, then the lab.
    • v24 puts everything on one scroll in the converged look: demo, +10% table, three reasons, lab and glossary. It was too long.
    • v25 cuts v24 to the one-minute path: the answer, the demo, why products differ, why it matters, and an optional lab.

Versions

Final choice

v25 is my final choice because a visitor sets a price and immediately sees buyers react. That taught elasticity faster than any read-only version, and it's what I valued most when I picked v06. It's also the clearest: a visitor understands in under a minute that demand reacts to price, that some products react more than others, and why that matters. I gave up some of the visual boldness of the early versions for clarity.
